0. Window order and time budget
-------------------------------
One boot plus the per-boot list in section 2 is about 55 min. Add about 10 min for a boot that
also runs quality --full.
  P0  preflight, both nodes                                         20 min   must
  P1  NCCL twin device check, serve down (only needed for KP)       45 min   should (before P5)
  P2  bundle interleave A, K, KC, A, K, KC (6 boots)                5.5 h    must
  P3  profile boots A_p and K_p (attribution by mechanism)           1.5 h    must
  P4  Viterbi pack arm CUR, VIT, CUR, VIT (promoted config)          4 h      must
  P5  KP safety boot, then K, KP, K, KP (5 boots)                    4.5 h    should
  P6  SPARSE_MARKOV: W, S, W, S, then S, S1024, S, S1024 (8 boots)  7.5 h    could
If the window is short, run P2, then P4, then P3, and move the rest to the next window. After a
promotion, the next pair's baseline carries the promoted envs (ARMS.md: "after a promotion the
next pair's A carries the promoted lever").

2. Per-boot procedure (identical on every boot, every arm; ARMS.md step 5 order)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  L=<arm>-<n>; O=$W/results/2026-09-2X-serve-r3/$L; mkdir -p $O; cd $W
  ./stop.sh                                          # both nodes; then the section 1 checks
  git rev-parse HEAD > $O/commit.txt; sha256sum docker/patch/* > $O/patch.sha256 2>/dev/null
  env AUDIT=strict IMAGE=$IMG <arm env> ./run.sh 2>&1 | tee $O/boot.txt
      # AUDIT=strict fails the boot unless every armed lever's LOG_ENGAGED line appears on BOTH
      # ranks with no LOG_DISARMED line (tools/engagement_audit.py). The lines are listed in
      # section 3.
  $S/gpu_guard.sh local  $O/gpu-spark1.txt 2  & G1=$!   # spark1 sampled every 2 s (Chromium)
  $S/gpu_guard.sh spark2 $O/gpu-spark2.txt 10 & G2=$!
  python3 smoke_chat.py  | tee $O/smoke_chat.txt       # non-empty content, 323
  python3 smoke_vision.py | tee $O/smoke_vision.txt    # no HTTP 400
  free -h > $O/mem-after-smoke.txt; ssh spark2 free -h >> $O/mem-after-smoke.txt   # >= 8 GiB
  tests/correctness.sh | tee $O/correctness.txt        # 8/8
  python3 tools/measure_lail_prose.py --runs 10 | tee $O/lail10.txt   # fresh L.A.I.L, before any c=2
  python3 bench_decode.py --runs 9 | tee $O/bench_decode.txt           # prose/structured c=1, c=2
  tools/four_numbers.sh --arm $L --out $O/four
  python3 benches/e2e.py | tee $O/e2e.txt              # exit 0
  <quality on the boots named in section 10, serialized, never during perf capture>
  tools/disarm_scan.sh > $O/disarm_scan.txt; echo "rc=$?" >> $O/disarm_scan.txt   # rc=0: no lever turned itself off
  kill $G1 $G2; $S/gpu_guard.sh --check $O/gpu-spark1.txt $O/gpu-spark2.txt > $O/gpu_guard.txt; \
    echo "rc=$?" >> $O/gpu_guard.txt                    # rc=0: no foreign GPU process
  ./stop.sh
Contamination rule. If gpu_guard --check exits 1 (a FOREIGN process on either GPU at any sample
between the smokes and the last bench), then touch $O/CONTAMINATED. That boot's numbers are void:
do not pool them, do not quote them. Re-run the same arm at once to keep the interleave order.
The profile stage showed why this matters: with Chromium on spark1's GPU, rank 0 straggles and
the whole TP=2 step slows (profile section 5, item 10). A 2 s sample can miss a short burst, so a
boot whose spread is out of family with its twins is suspect even when the guard is clean.

5. Phase P2: kernel bundle, three-arm interleave (6 boots)
---------------------------------------------------------
  A:  env AUDIT=strict IMAGE=$IMG ./run.sh                    (the round-34 defaults, round-3 levers off)
  K:  env AUDIT=strict IMAGE=$IMG $K_ENV ./run.sh
  KC: env AUDIT=strict IMAGE=$IMG $KC_ENV ./run.sh            (K without COOP)
  Order: A#1, K#1, KC#1, A#2, K#2, KC#2. Each arm boots twice, interleaved (ARMS.md ABAB extended to a
  cycle). The boots give three noise-gated comparisons:
    K  vs A   the bundle (promotion decision)
    K  vs KC  COOP alone. It is the largest lever, and the only one whose numerics are not
              bit-exact (fp32 slot-sum order, <= 1 fp16 ulp), so acceptance and quality are
              attributed separately for it.
    KC vs A   the bit-exact rest (DENSE_GEMV + MHC_DET + the 7 fusion-host levers)
Primary metric: prose_median_ms_per_step and prose_long c=1 median_ms_per_step, at matched
acceptance. The per-boot median_run_acceptance_len must overlap between the compared arms. For
KC vs A this is guaranteed by construction (bit-exact), so a gap means something is wrong: stop
and diff. For K vs KC a small move is possible (COOP <= 1 ulp). If it does not overlap, gate on
prose_long c=1 tok/s and L.A.I.L tok/s instead (ARMS.md step 6).
Promote K (all 10 envs default-on) only when all of these hold:
- The K-vs-A median improvement exceeds the noise (the larger boot-to-boot spread of A and K).
- Every K boot beats the A median.
- Non-inferiority on pp_warm / pp_novel 8k/32k, prose_long c=2 aggregate and warm-prefix.
- Honest cells: natural_finish_reason length, serve_env_ranks_match true, lever_disarmed false.
- disarm_scan is clean and gpu_guard is clean on every counted boot.
- Quality quick passes on both K boots and quality --full passes on K#2 (section 10).
If K vs KC is inside the noise, keep COOP in the bundle only if its mechanism shows in P3 (below).
If K vs KC is worse beyond the noise, drop COOP from K.
Expected, c=1 (reference: R34 s13 prose_long c=1 62.88 ms/step at 33.13 tok/s; k3 profile boot 1
L.A.I.L 62.8 ms/step):
    K vs A   -11.9 .. -13.1 ms/step  (to ~49.8-51.0 ms/step; ~+23-26% tok/s at matched acceptance)
    K vs KC  -6.8 (6.76-6.81 +- 0.16)
    KC vs A  -5.1 .. -6.3
  c=2: K vs A about -21.5 .. -22.0 ms/step (COOP -15.2 .. -15.3 of it).

12. Interactions and non-additivity (read before judging a bundle against the sum)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- DENSE_GEMV x WKV_TP: dense-gemv tunes (K, N) = (6144, 25600) for Engram wkv. Under WKV_TP the
  per-rank shape is (6144, 12800), which has no dense-gemv config, so that GEMM stays on b12x and
  dense-gemv's wkv share (~57 us per call, ~0.11 ms/step) is lost. WKV_TP's own saving is larger.
  Follow-up: tune a (6144, 12800) config.
- MHC_OVERLAP x L2_PREFETCH: both fork a side stream at the MoE all-reduce and read DRAM inside the
  window (1.23 MB of packed fn vs up to 10 MiB of qkv_a). Judge them together in KP_p. mhc-det's
  plan predates comm's move to a 10 MiB default; if the AR median rises, try
  DSV41_AR_L2_PREFETCH_MIB=5.5.
- COOP x MOE_PREP_FUSED: the fused prep feeds p2b the same inputs, bit-exact against the stock
  glue, and COOP reads them unchanged. MOE_PREP_FUSED already covers most of coop-moe's planned
  "fold the wrapper glue" lever (input prep in one kernel, one output cast). Measure the remaining
  gap in K_p before building that lever.
- DENSE_GEMV x L2_PREFETCH: they stack. In the L2 regime the GEMV still wins +19-20% at qkv_a M<=4
  and +9.1-9.7% at M 6/8 (dense-gemv/l2-regime-1.json).
- EAGER_TWIN depends on PM_QOS: without it, the graph communicator's proxy thread can wake from
  deep idle at each replay start (up to one more LPI-3 exit, ~0.43 ms). That is why both are in KP.
- Rank skew: the k3 profile found rank 0 late on 72% of all-reduces (1.34 / 3.92 ms/step of wait),
  in a window that may have had Chromium on spark1. With COOP shortening the MoE, the skew can move
  either way. Re-read it in K_p with gpu_guard clean.

13. Rollback
------------
All round-3 levers are env-gated and off by default, so rollback is ./stop.sh and a boot without
the envs. The image can stay (review-e14 with the envs unset runs canonical-e13's kernels); or
boot IMAGE=dsv41-flash-exl3-sm121:canonical-e13, the current IMAGE default. Promotion itself means:
- the promoted envs become defaults in recipe.yaml, then kit/render.py;
- recipe.yaml's image tag moves to the e14 content (docker tag review-e14 canonical-e14 on both
  nodes, same ID);
- verdicts go to flags.md and results/RESULTS.md (ARMS.md step 8).
