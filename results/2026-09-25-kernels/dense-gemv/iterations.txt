dense-gemv iteration log (k3, 2026-09-25)
==========================================
Machine: spark2 GB10 (sm_121a, 48 SMs, 24 MiB L2), image dsv41-flash-exl3-sm121:canonical-e13,
microbench only, spark2 GPU under .worktrees-spark2/.gpu.lock, pmon checked before every run
(only Xorg/gnome-shell with no SM use were ever present). SM clock 2190 MHz in every run
(runs/*.clocks.csv). Numbers are cold medians (us) unless noted; b12x = production
FlashInfer mm_mxfp8(auto) on SM121 (tile 16x64 / 16x128, no swap), b12x_e2e = FlashInfer
mxfp8_quantize (cute-dsl) + b12x, the serve's call from a bf16 activation.

Numerics decision (made before any timing): reproduce b12x bit for bit. Same activation
MXFP8 quant rule (FlashInfer cute-dsl mxfp8_quantize PTX, copied verbatim), same
mma.sync m16n8k32 kind::mxf8f6f4 block_scale scale_vec::1X e4m3 x e4m3 ue8m0 instruction,
k blocks chained into one fp32 accumulator in increasing k from 0.0, bf16 RN output. No
split-K (it would reorder the chain). Why: bitwise-equal output is quality-neutral by
construction (no dNLL gate needed, serve A/B is perf-only); bf16 activations would change
the numerics of ~230 GEMMs/step and need the quality harness, and they double the
activation bytes and need e4m3->bf16 converts in the inner loop. The MMA is issued swap-AB
(weights = 16-row A operand, M<=8 activation rows = n8 B operand); measured bitwise equal
to b12x's A=activation orientation on every case below.

Protocol lessons (benchutil.py docstring has the final protocol):
 P1 A linear read-flush left the buffer placed right after the flush buffer warm
    (75.8 vs 95.2 us for 21 MB): stream prefetch past the flush. -> hashed-order flush.
 P2 Even hashed, a buffer re-read every iteration stayed partly cached (274-284 GB/s > the
    273 GB/s DRAM peak) in some placements: scan-resistant L2/SLC replacement keeps re-hit
    lines. -> every arm rotates over >= 64 distinct copies of REAL layer weights
    (>= 512 MiB), arms read different copies per iteration, double 64 MiB hashed flush
    before every timed call. With that no arm ever exceeded 237 GB/s.
 P3 Event timestamps have ~1 us granularity on GB10 (1.024 us steps) -> medians of 200
    samples + p10/p90 + mean; A/B arms interleaved (ABBA) in one process.
 P4 In the serve the activation was just written by the previous kernel: the cold
    protocol re-touches the activation after the flush (weights cold, activation L2-hot).

Calibration (calib2.py, pure read of the weight bytes, cold): best read us / GB/s
  shared_down 5.90 MB 28.7 (206) | qkv_a 9.18 MB 43.0 (214) | gate_up 11.8 MB 53.3 (222)
  wq_b/wo_b 21.0 MB 92.2 (228) | main_proj 78.6 MB 332 (237) | engram 157 MB 665 (237)
  lm_head 331 MB 1396 (237). Short kernels pay ~4 us of ramp+tail.
persm.py: one SM pulls 56 GB/s with 64-128 KB of LDG in flight; 4 SMs x 64 KB reach
  226 GB/s -> loaded DRAM latency ~1.1 us, ~256 KB in flight saturates the chip.
ringcal.py: a per-warp cp.async ring fetching 16 rows x KC contiguous bytes per stage
  streams at the flat-read roofline once KC >= 512 (qkv_a 43.07 vs flat 42.98; wo_b 93.2
  vs 92.2); KC = 128 (v1/v2) loses 15-30% (short per-row bursts over ~1800 row streams).
